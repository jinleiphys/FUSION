# Effects of anisotropic pressure on interacting quark star structure

> Perturbative Quantum Chromodynamics (pQCD) corrections and color superconductivity predict that strongly interacting matter can reveal new physical phenomena under extreme conditions. Taking into account these interaction effects, we investigate the role of anisotropic pressure in quark stars composed of interacting quark matter. Adopting two physically well-motivated anisotropy profiles, we numerically solve the stellar structure equations in order to explore the consequences of anisotropic pressure on various macroscopic properties such as radius, gravitational mass, surface redshift, moment of inertia, tidal Love number and oscillation spectrum. Remarkably, for both anisotropy models, negative anisotropies increase the radial stability of interacting quark stars, while the opposite occurs for positive anisotropies. However, for the Bowers-Liang profile, the central density corresponding to the maximum-mass point does not coincide with the central density where the squared oscillation frequency vanishes, indicating that the existence of stable anisotropic interacting quark stars is possible beyond the maximum mass for negative anisotropies. Additionally, we compare our theoretical predictions with several observational mass-radius measurements and tidal deformability constraints, which suggest that both strong interaction effects and anisotropy effects play a crucial role in describing compact stars observed in the Universe.

## Key claim

For the Bowers-Liang anisotropy profile, the central density at the maximum-mass point does not coincide with the central density where the squared radial oscillation frequency vanishes, meaning that stable anisotropic interacting quark stars can exist beyond the maximum mass for negative anisotropies, contrary to the usual expectation that the maximum-mass point marks the onset of instability.

## Method

The authors numerically solved the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) equations for static, spherically symmetric quark stars using an interacting quark matter equation of state that includes perturbative QCD corrections and color superconductivity, parametrized by a dimensionless parameter \(\bar{\lambda}\). They adopted two anisotropy profiles: the quasi-local model of Horvat et al. (2010) and the Bowers-Liang (1974) model, and computed macroscopic properties including mass, radius, moment of inertia, tidal Love number, and radial oscillation frequencies for varying anisotropy parameters.

## Key numbers

- For the isotropic case with \(B_{\rm eff}= 60\, \rm MeV/fm^3\) and \(\bar{\lambda}=10\) at central density \(\rho_c= 0.5 \times 10^{18}\, \rm kg/m^3\): radius \(R = 18.358\, \rm km\), mass \(M = 3.886\, M_\odot\), surface redshift \(z_{\rm sur}=0.633\), moment of inertia \(I = 1.441 \times 10^{39}\, \rm kg \cdot m^2\), tidal Love number \(k_2 = 0.040\), fundamental mode frequency \(f_0 = 1.323\, \rm kHz\), first overtone \(f_1 = 4.787\, \rm kHz\).

- For the quasi-local model with \(\beta_{\rm H}= -0.6\) at the same central density: \(R = 17.850\, \rm km\), \(M = 3.473\, M_\odot\), \(z_{\rm sur}=0.533\), \(I = 1.157 \times 10^{39}\, \rm kg \cdot m^2\), \(k_2 = 0.061\), \(f_0 = 1.586\, \rm kHz\), \(f_1 = 5.576\, \rm kHz\).

- For the quasi-local model with \(\beta_{\rm H}= 0.6\) at the same central density: \(R = 18.785\, \rm km\), \(M = 4.327\, M_\odot\), \(z_{\rm sur}=0.768\), \(I = 1.788 \times 10^{39}\, \rm kg \cdot m^2\), \(k_2 = 0.025\), \(f_0 = 0.993\, \rm kHz\), \(f_1 = 3.971\, \rm kHz\).

- For the Bowers-Liang model with \(\beta_{\rm BL}= -0.30\) at the same central density: \(R = 17.818\, \rm km\), \(M = 3.519\, M_\odot\), \(z_{\rm sur}=0.549\), \(I = 1.170 \times 10^{39}\, \rm kg \cdot m^2\), \(k_2 = 0.052\), \(f_0 = 1.722\, \rm kHz\), \(f_1 = 5.543\, \rm kHz\).

- For the Bowers-Liang model with \(\beta_{\rm BL}= 0.30\) at the same central density: \(R = 18.916\, \rm km\), \(M = 4.305\, M_\odot\), \(z_{\rm sur}=0.747\), \(I = 1.798 \times 10^{39}\, \rm kg \cdot m^2\), \(k_2 = 0.029\), \(f_0 = 0.875\, \rm kHz\), \(f_1 = 4.002\, \rm kHz\).

## Context

This work extends the study of anisotropic compact stars by Bowers and Liang (1974) and Horvat et al. (2010) to the specific case of interacting quark matter with pQCD corrections and color superconductivity, building on the equation of state parametrized by Zhang (2020, 2021). It enables future comparisons with observational mass-radius measurements from NICER (Miller 2019) and tidal deformability constraints from gravitational wave events such as GW170817, and suggests that anisotropic effects can alter the stability criteria for quark stars beyond the standard maximum-mass turning point.
