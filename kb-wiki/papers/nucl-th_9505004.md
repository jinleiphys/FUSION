# The Role of Strangeness in Ultrarelativistic Nuclear Collisions

> We review the progress in understanding the strange particle yields in nuclear collisions and their role in signalling quark-gluon plasma formation. We report on new insights into the formation mechanisms of strange particles during ultrarelativistic heavy-ion collisions and discuss interesting new details of the strangeness phase diagram. In the main part of the review we show how the measured (multi-)strange particle abundances can be used as a testing ground for chemical equilibration in nuclear collisions, and how the results of such an analysis lead to important constraints on the collision dynamics and space-time evolution of high energy heavy-ion reactions.

## Key claim

Strange particle abundances in ultrarelativistic nuclear collisions can serve as a quantitative test of chemical equilibration, and the resulting analysis constrains the collision dynamics and space-time evolution of the reaction, but the review emphasizes that this interpretation depends on the assumption that the measured yields reflect equilibrium conditions rather than non-equilibrium production mechanisms.

## Method

The paper reviews experimental data on (multi-)strange particle yields from ultrarelativistic heavy-ion collisions and compares them to predictions from chemical equilibrium models. It examines the strangeness phase diagram and the formation mechanisms of strange particles, using the measured abundances to test whether chemical equilibration is achieved in the collision fireball.

## Context

This review extends earlier work on strangeness production as a signature of quark-gluon plasma formation, building on the framework of chemical equilibration analyses. It enables future experimental programs to use multi-strange baryon yields as a diagnostic tool for the collision dynamics and the space-time evolution of high-energy heavy-ion reactions. The paper discusses the strangeness phase diagram and formation mechanisms, situating itself within the ongoing effort to identify quark-gluon plasma signals in nuclear collisions.
