# Ab initio calculations on nuclear matter properties including the effects of three-nucleons interaction

> In this thesis, the ground state properties of nuclear matter, namely the energy per particle and the response to weak probes, are computed, studying the effects of three nucleon interactions. Both the variational approach, based on the formalism of correlated basis function, and the auxiliary field diffusion Monte Carlo method have been used. A scheme suitable to construct a density-dependent two-nucleon potential in correlated basis approach is discussed. The density dependent potential resulting from UIX three-nucleon force has been employed in auxiliary field diffusion Monte Carlo calculations that turned out to be in very good agreement with correlated basis variational results. Hence, the underbinding of symmetric nuclear matter has to be ascribed to deficiencies of the UXI potential. A comparative analysis of the equations of state of both pure neutron matter and symmetric nuclear matter obtained using a new generation of "chiral inspired" local three-body potentials has been performed. These potentials provide an excellent description of the properties of light nuclei, as well as of the neutron-deuteron doublet scattering length. The weak response of symmetric nuclear matter has been computed at three-body cluster level. Two-body effective interactions and one-body effective operators have been derived within the formalism of correlated basis functions. The inclusion of the three-body cluster term in the effective interaction allowed for a direct inclusion of the UIX three-nucleon potential. Moreover, the sizable unphysical dependence of the effective weak operator is removed once the three-body cluster term is taken into account.

## Key claim
The underbinding of symmetric nuclear matter is attributed to deficiencies of the UIX three-nucleon potential, not to the computational method, as the auxiliary field diffusion Monte Carlo results using a density-dependent potential derived from UIX are in very good agreement with correlated basis variational results.

## Method
The ground state properties of nuclear matter were computed using both the correlated basis function variational approach and the auxiliary field diffusion Monte Carlo method. A scheme was developed to construct a density-dependent two-nucleon potential from the UIX three-nucleon force for use in the Monte Carlo calculations. The weak response of symmetric nuclear matter was computed at the three-body cluster level, deriving two-body effective interactions and one-body effective operators within the correlated basis function formalism.

## Key numbers
- The weak response of symmetric nuclear matter was computed at the three-body cluster level, where the inclusion of the three-body cluster term removed a "sizable unphysical dependence" of the effective weak operator.
- The density-dependent potential resulting from the UIX three-nucleon force was employed in auxiliary field diffusion Monte Carlo calculations, which "turned out to be in very good agreement with correlated basis variational results."
- The "chiral inspired" local three-body potentials provided "an excellent description of the properties of light nuclei, as well as of the neutron-deuteron doublet scattering length."

## Context
This work extends the correlated basis function formalism and auxiliary field diffusion Monte Carlo methods to include the effects of three-nucleon interactions in nuclear matter, building on prior variational approaches. The comparative analysis of equations of state using new "chiral inspired" local three-body potentials connects the study to modern effective field theory developments. The computation of the weak response at the three-body cluster level, which removes unphysical dependencies found in earlier two-body cluster calculations (Cowell 2003), enables more reliable predictions for neutrino-nucleus interactions in dense matter.
