# Effects of in-medium vector meson masses on low-mass dileptons from SPS heavy-ion collisions

> Using a relativistic transport model to describe the expansion of the fire-cylinder formed in the initial stage of heavy-ion collisions at SPS/CERN energies, we study the production of dileptons with mass below about 1 GeV from these collisions. The initial hadron abundance and their momentum distributions in the fire-cylinder are determined by following the general features of the results from microscopic models based on the string dynamics and further requiring that the final proton and pion spectra and rapidity distributions are in agreement with available experimental data. For dilepton production, we include the Dalitz decay of $π^0$, $η$, $η^\prime$, $ω$ and $a_1$ mesons, the direct decay of primary $ρ^0$, $ω$ and $ϕ$ mesons, and the pion-pion annihilation that proceeds through the $ρ^0$ meson, the pion-rho annihilation that proceeds through the $a_1$ meson, and the kaon-antikaon annihilation that proceeds through the $ϕ$ meson. We find that the modification of vector meson properties, especially the decrease of their mass due to the partial restoration of chiral symmetry, in hot and dense hadronic matter, provides a quantitative explanation of the recently observed enhancement of low-mass dileptons by the CERES collaboration in central S+Au collisions and by the HELIOS-3 collaboration in central S+W collisions.

## Key claim

The paper claims that the observed enhancement of low-mass dileptons (invariant mass between 250 MeV and 1 GeV) in central S+Au and S+W collisions at SPS/CERN energies can be quantitatively explained by the decrease of vector meson masses in hot and dense hadronic matter due to partial restoration of chiral symmetry, but this explanation depends on the specific initial conditions of the fire-cylinder and the particular model used for in-medium mass modifications.

## Method

The authors used a relativistic transport model to describe the expansion of a fire-cylinder formed in the initial stage of heavy-ion collisions at SPS/CERN energies, with initial hadron abundances and momentum distributions constrained by fitting final proton and pion spectra and rapidity distributions to experimental data from the NA35 and NA44 collaborations. They included dilepton production from Dalitz decays of π⁰, η, η′, ω, and a₁ mesons, direct decays of primary ρ⁰, ω, and φ mesons, and annihilation processes (ππ→ρ⁰, πρ→a₁, K¯K→φ), using an extended Walecka model to couple light quarks of hadrons to scalar and vector fields for consistent treatment of in-medium mass modifications.

## Key numbers

- Initial baryon density: about 2.5ρ₀ (ρ₀ = 0.16 fm⁻³), with baryon number in initial fire-cylinder about 100 (32 from projectile, 68 from target)
- Initial temperature: about 165 MeV, determined by fitting slopes of proton and pion transverse momentum spectra
- Initial fire-cylinder volume: about 250 fm⁻³, with cross section about 40 fm² and longitudinal length 6.2 fm
- In the free mass case: initial π, ρ, ω, and a₁ meson numbers were 119, 56, 43, and 10 respectively, with initial pion chemical potential about 130 MeV
- In the medium-modified mass case: initial π, ρ, ω, and a₁ meson numbers were 44, 92, 33, and 26 respectively, with zero pion chemical potential
- At initial temperature 165 MeV and baryon density 2.5ρ₀: rho and omega meson masses reduced to about 270 MeV; a₁ meson mass reduced to about 550 MeV; η meson mass reduced to about 210 MeV; η′ meson mass reduced to about 810 MeV
- Initial η and η′ numbers: 37 and 5 in free mass case; 26 and 3 in medium-modified mass case
- Final η number at freeze-out: about 32 in both cases
- Freeze-out time: t = 20 fm/c

## Context

This work extends the authors' previous study (Li, Ko, and Brown, 1995) by including η, η′, φ, and a₁ mesons omitted in the earlier Letter, and by using the original Walecka model instead of the nonlinear Walecka model to lower initial baryon density from unrealistically high values. The paper provides a quantitative explanation for the low-mass dilepton enhancement observed by the CERES collaboration in central S+Au collisions at 200 GeV/nucleon and by the HELIOS-3 collaboration in central S+W collisions, which could not be explained by uncertainties in normalization procedures (Tserruya). The approach contrasts with lattice QCD simulations (Boyd et al., 1995) showing no significant change of rho-meson screening mass up to 0.92T_c, and with effective Lagrangian calculations (Song) showing rho meson mass unchanged at leading order in temperature, instead supporting the Brown-Rho scaling hypothesis based on restoration of scale invariance. The results enable further experimental tests at SIS/GSI by the HADES collaboration and at higher energies, and provide a framework for studying the transition from hadronic matter to quark-gluon plasma through dilepton observables.
