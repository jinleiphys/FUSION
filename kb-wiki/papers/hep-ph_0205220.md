# Detection of Supernova Neutrinos by Neutrino-Proton Elastic Scattering

> We propose that neutrino-proton elastic scattering, $ν+ p \to ν+ p$, can be used for the detection of supernova neutrinos in scintillator detectors. Though the proton recoil kinetic energy spectrum is soft, with $T_p \simeq 2 E_ν^2/M_p$, and the scintillation light output from slow, heavily ionizing protons is quenched, the yield above a realistic threshold is nearly as large as that from $\barν_e + p \to e^+ + n$. In addition, the measured proton spectrum is related to the incident neutrino spectrum, which solves a long-standing problem of how to separately measure the total energy and temperature of $ν_μ$, $ν_τ$, $\barν_μ$, and $\barν_τ$. The ability to detect this signal would give detectors like KamLAND and Borexino a crucial and unique role in the quest to detect supernova neutrinos.

## Key claim

The authors propose that neutrino-proton elastic scattering (ν + p → ν + p) can serve as a viable detection channel for supernova neutrinos in scintillator detectors, and crucially that the measured proton recoil spectrum allows separate determination of the total energy and temperature of ν_μ, ν_τ, anti-ν_μ, and anti-ν_τ, a long-standing problem in supernova neutrino detection, provided the detector threshold is low enough to overcome the soft proton recoil spectrum and scintillation light quenching.

## Method

The authors calculated the differential cross section for neutrino-proton elastic scattering using Standard Model neutral-current couplings, then convolved this with Fermi-Dirac neutrino spectra characteristic of supernova models (temperatures of 3.5, 5, and 8 MeV for different flavors). They applied Birk's Law with a quenching constant k_B = 0.015 cm/MeV (appropriate for KamLAND scintillator) to convert true proton kinetic energies into observable electron-equivalent energies, and performed Monte Carlo simulations with chi-squared fits to determine how well the total energy and temperature of the four heavy-flavor neutrino species could be extracted from the measured proton spectrum.

## Key numbers

- The total cross section for neutrino-proton elastic scattering is approximately 4 times smaller than that for charged-current anti-ν_e + p → e+ + n, with form G_F^2 E_ν^2 (c_V^2 + 3c_A^2)/π where c_V = 0.04 and c_A = 1.27/2
- For a standard supernova at 10 kpc in a 1 kton KamLAND detector, the total yield from neutrino-proton elastic scattering above zero threshold is 624 events, compared to approximately 310 events from anti-ν_e + p → e+ + n
- Above a 0.2 MeV electron-equivalent threshold (corresponding to 1.2 MeV true proton kinetic energy), the yield from the sum of ν_μ, ν_τ, anti-ν_μ, and anti-ν_τ (T = 8 MeV) is 253 events (127 + 126), while ν_e (T = 3.5 MeV) yields only 3 events and anti-ν_e (T = 5 MeV) yields 17 events
- The proton quenching factor (E_equiv/T_p) is approximately 1/2 at 10 MeV, 1/3 at 6 MeV, and 1/4 at 3 MeV
- The thermally-averaged cross section for anti-ν_e + p → e+ + n at T = 5 MeV is 44 × 10^{-42} cm^2 (for 2 protons)

## Context

This work extends the problem of measuring supernova neutrino spectra from neutral-current reactions, which had previously been considered impossible because most neutral-current channels (such as ν + d → ν + p + n in SNO or nuclear excitations) provide no information on the incident neutrino energy. The authors show that neutrino-proton elastic scattering in scintillator detectors like KamLAND (already operating) and Borexino (soon to operate) provides a unique solution, enabling separate measurement of the total energy and temperature of the heavy-flavor neutrinos that carry about 2/3 of the supernova binding energy. This capability is crucial for determining the neutron star mass from the total energy and for studying neutrino oscillations from the temperature, without requiring model-dependent assumptions as in prior approaches.
