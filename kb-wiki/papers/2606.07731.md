# NICER Constraints on Low density Interpolation and High density Continuation in Neutron Star Equations of State

> We investigate whether current astrophysical data constrain not only the high density continuation of the neutron star equation of state, but also the low density matching procedure itself. To this end, we compare two low density branches propagated through a common high density extension and confront them with direct NICER mass-radius posteriors, a lower bound on the maximum mass, and an effective constraint on $Λ_{1.4}$. We find that the observable predictions of the two branches remain strongly overlapping, while the NICER-informed posterior still induces a nontrivial constraint on the matching parameters. Current data therefore constrain primarily the shared continuation above $n_1$, but also indirectly restrict the low-density matching sector.

# NICER Constraints on Low density Interpolation and High density Continuation in Neutron Star Equations of State

## Key claim
Current astrophysical data primarily constrain the shared high-density continuation above n₁ = 0.32 fm⁻³, but also indirectly restrict the low-density matching sector through a nontrivial posterior constraint on the matching parameters (n_m, Δ_m), even though the observable predictions of different low-density constructions remain strongly overlapping.

## Method
Two alternative low-density baseline branches were constructed below n₁ = 0.32 fm⁻³: branch A smoothly matches a low-density β-equilibrated input to a β-stable χEFT branch via a hyperbolic tangent interpolation, while branch B continues the low-density input directly up to n₁ as a control. Both branches were extended above n₁ with an identical parametrized high-density continuation consisting of a polytropic segment up to a transition density n₂ followed by a causal speed-of-sound branch. The full stellar sequences were confronted with direct NICER mass-radius posteriors for PSR J0030+0451 and PSR J0740+6620, a hard lower bound on maximum mass (M_max ≥ 2.0 M_⊙), and an effective constraint on Λ₁.₄, using a coarse Latin-hypercube exploration followed by posterior-guided refinement.

## Key numbers
- n₁ = 0.32 fm⁻³: the density threshold below which the two low-density branches differ and above which the common continuation is applied
- Matching parameters for branch A: n_m = 0.1798⁺⁰·⁰⁴¹⁷₋₀.₀₅₁₃ fm⁻³ (median with 68% credible interval), Δ_m = 0.0207⁺⁰·⁰¹¹⁴₋₀.₀₁₀₆ fm⁻³
- 95% credible intervals for matching parameters: n_m ∈ [0.1010, 0.2426] fm⁻³, Δ_m ∈ [0.0054, 0.0403] fm⁻³
- Common continuation parameters for branch A (68% CI): n₂ = 0.4846⁺⁰·¹¹⁵⁴₋₀.₀₈₂₅ fm⁻³, Γ₁ = 4.0271⁺⁰·⁶³¹⁷₋₀.₄₂₉₇
- Common continuation parameters for branch B (68% CI): n₂ = 0.4752⁺⁰·¹¹⁷²₋₀.₀₇₁₃ fm⁻³, Γ₁ = 4.0568⁺⁰·⁶⁷⁷⁹₋₀.₅₁₅₉
- c_s,cap² for branch A (68% CI): 0.7673⁺⁰·¹⁴²⁶₋₀.₁₅₇₁; for branch B: 0.7114⁺⁰·¹⁶²⁰₋₀.₂₀₇₂
- Hard lower bound on maximum mass: M_max ≥ 2.0 M_⊙
- Effective tidal constraint: Λ₁.₄ = 350 ± 120 (Gaussian proxy), with broad requirement 1 ≤ Λ₁.₄ ≤ 2000

## Context
This work extends the modular equation-of-state framework of the MUSES Calculation Engine (PhysRevD.111.103037, 2025) by statistically calibrating the matching freedom between low-density and χEFT descriptions, building on the χEFT calculations of Machleidt and Entem (2011) and the crust-DFT approach of Lim and Holt (2017). It directly uses the updated NICER mass-radius posteriors for PSR J0030+0451 from Kini et al. (2026) and PSR J0740+6620 from Dittmann et al. (2024), together with the massive pulsar constraints from Antoniadis et al. (2013) and Fonseca et al. (2021). The analysis enables future studies with explicit composition-dependent core models (npeμ, octet-enriched, decuplet-enriched, and quark-matter sectors) by establishing which parts of a modular multi-region construction are already constrained by current observations, and by providing a statistical criterion for selecting viable low-density matching prescriptions before introducing more complex core physics.
