# Astrophysical constraints on nuclear EOSs and coupling constants in RMF models

> Utilizing various astrophysical constraints on neutron star structures, we carry out a Bayesian analysis on the density-dependent behaviors of coupling constants in RMF models as well as the nuclear matter properties at supranuclear densities. The effective nucleon interactions in the isoscalar-scalar, isoscalar-vector, and isovector-vector channels are considered, where the corresponding coupling constants ($α_S, α_V, α_{TV}$) are fixed by dividing entire density range into three regions with six independent parameters. In this work we focus on constraining the density-dependent point-coupling constants at supranuclear densities, while the coupling constants at subsaturation densities are derived from the covariant density functional DD-ME2. For those consistent with astrophysical observations, the coupling constants generally decrease with density and approach to small positive values at large enough densities, which qualitatively agrees with various RMF models. The posterior probability density functions and their correlations of the coupling constants and various nuclear matter properties are examined as well. At $1σ$ level, the constrained coupling constants at density $1.5n_0$ ($2.5n_0$) are $α_S = 3.1^{+0.1}_{-0.05} (1.55^{+0.85}_{-0.2}) \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{MeV}^{-2}$, $α_V = 2.3^{+0.1}_{-0.0} (1.3^{+0.55}_{-0.1}) \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{MeV}^{-2}$, and $α_{TV} = 2.05^{+0}_{-0.4} (2.05^{+0}_{-0.5})\times 10^{-5} \mathrm{MeV}^{-2}$. At larger densities, we find the lower limit of $α_{TV}$ is not well constrained, so that more extensive calculations with larger number of free parameters are necessary.

## Key claim
The density-dependent coupling constants in relativistic mean-field (RMF) models, when constrained by astrophysical neutron star observations, generally decrease with density and approach small positive values at large densities, qualitatively agreeing with various RMF models; however, the lower limit of the isovector-vector coupling constant α_TV is not well constrained at larger densities, indicating that more extensive calculations with a larger number of free parameters are necessary.

## Method
The authors performed a Bayesian analysis using Markov-Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) with the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm to constrain six independent parameters governing the density-dependent point-coupling constants (α_S, α_V, α_TV) in the isoscalar-scalar, isoscalar-vector, and isovector-vector channels at supranuclear densities. The density range was divided into three regions (0.1 fm⁻³ to n₀, n₀ to 2n₀, and above 2n₀), with coupling constants at subsaturation densities fixed by the covariant density functional DD-ME2 (Lalazissis 2005). The resulting equations of state were used to solve the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff equations, and the likelihood was constructed from neutron star radius data from GW170817, quiescent low-mass X-ray binaries, NICER observations of PSR J0030+0451 and PSR J0740+6620, and HESS J1731-347, along with a maximum mass filter of 1.97 M⊙ and causality/thermodynamic stability conditions.

## Key numbers
- At 1σ level, coupling constants at density 1.5n₀: α_S = 3.1⁺⁰·¹₋₀.₀₅ × 10⁻⁴ MeV⁻², α_V = 2.3⁺⁰·¹₋₀.₀ × 10⁻⁴ MeV⁻², α_TV = 2.05⁺⁰₋₀.₄ × 10⁻⁵ MeV⁻².
- At 1σ level, coupling constants at density 2.5n₀: α_S = 1.55⁺⁰·⁸⁵₋₀.₂ × 10⁻⁴ MeV⁻², α_V = 1.3⁺⁰·⁵⁵₋₀.₁ × 10⁻⁴ MeV⁻², α_TV = 2.05⁺⁰₋₀.₅ × 10⁻⁵ MeV⁻².
- Constrained pressure of neutron star matter at 1.5n₀: P = 10⁺⁰·⁰₋₀.₀ MeV/fm³; at 2.5n₀: P = 60⁺¹⁵₋₂₀ MeV/fm³.
- Constrained symmetry energy at 1.5n₀: ε_sym = 45⁺¹·⁰₋₃.₀ MeV; at 2.5n₀: ε_sym = 60⁺⁸·⁰₋₆.₀ MeV.
- 90% credible interval for maximum neutron star mass: 2.0⁺⁰·⁶⁵₋₀.₀ M⊙.
- For a 1.4 M⊙ neutron star: radius R = 13⁺⁰·³₋₀.₅ km, tidal deformability Λ = 605⁺¹⁵⁰₋₁₆₅, central energy density ε_c = 340⁺¹⁰⁰₋₃₀ MeV/fm³, central speed of sound v_s^c = 0.7⁺⁰·²₋₀.₁.
- For a 2.0 M⊙ neutron star: radius R = 13.3⁺⁰·³₋₁.₃ km, tidal deformability Λ = 75⁺¹⁵₋₅₀, central energy density ε_c = 420⁺⁴⁴⁰₋₅₀ MeV/fm³, central speed of sound v_s^c = 0.8⁺⁰·⁰⁵₋₀.₄.

## Context
This work extends Bayesian analyses of neutron star equations of state (e.g., Xie 2019, Xie 2020) by specifically constraining the density-dependent behavior of point-coupling constants in RMF models, rather than only the EOS itself. The results are consistent with the covariant density functional DD-ME2 (Lalazissis 2005) at subsaturation densities and qualitatively agree with other RMF models. The finding that neutron stars can reach 2.5 M⊙ while meeting current observations is relevant for interpreting the secondary objects in GW190814 and GW200210 (LVC 2020, Zhu 2022) and the hypothesized lower mass gap 2.5-5 M⊙ (Yang 2020). The authors note that the central compact object in HESS J1731-347 (Doroshenko 2022) shows no overlap with their predicted mass-radius distributions, suggesting it might be a strange star or other exotic object.
